# Your task

Decide which agent sounds more like a skilled human professional on the phone: the one a caller would more readily take for a real person who does this job well.

Listen for:

- **Spoken, not written.** No lists, headings, symbols or formatting read aloud. Numbers, dates, amounts, codes and addresses said the way people say them.
- **Turn shape.** Short turns that carry one thing at a time and leave room for the caller. No monologues.
- **Listening.** Reacting to what the caller actually said, acknowledging it naturally, not asking again for what was already given, and adapting when the caller corrects, interrupts or hesitates.
- **Warmth without a script.** Empathy and politeness that fit the moment, not stock phrases. Varied wording, not the same line again and again.
- **Language.** The caller's language and register as a local customer service person really speaks it, including mixing in English words where people really do.
- **The moments a person handles.** Filling a pause while looking something up, asking the caller to hold, handing over to a colleague and saying goodbye.

Ignore whether the agent completed the task, followed its business's rules or got its facts right: that is measured separately. Judge only how each agent sounds. Do not favour the longer call, the more polite agent or the one shown first.

Answer "tie" only when neither agent sounds more human than the other in any way a caller would notice.

Give your reasons in English, in two to four sentences, quoting the calls where it helps. Then give your verdict: "A", "B" or "tie".
